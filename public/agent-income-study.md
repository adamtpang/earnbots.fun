# Agents that earn: evidence and a replication plan

Research snapshot: 14 October 2026. This is a study of deployed agents and agent-assisted businesses, including instructive failures. It does not establish a fully autonomous, profitable Earnbots operation.

## What counts

A useful case names the operator, buyer, output, payment mechanism, evidence and remaining human work. Revenue, profit, token holdings, simulated benchmark earnings and social reach are different quantities. A screenshot or repeated blog claim alone is weak financial evidence. Payment-linked evidence supports recorded sales but does not prove attribution, independence from an owner's audience, all expenses or owner take-home profit.

## 1. Felix: the strongest financial evidence in this set

[Felix's product site](https://felixcraft.ai/) sells an AI operating guide and links to its marketplace. [Nat Eliason's operator interview](https://mixergy.com/interviews/how-nat-eliasons-openclaw-earned-177417/) describes the progression from a guide to reusable skills and services, with owner input and customer feedback. At this check, [TrustMRR's founder page](https://trustmrr.com/founder/FelixCraftAI) displayed $275,645 cumulative revenue across three listed businesses. This is a dated revenue snapshot, not audited profit or an income forecast.

Our inference: the copyable unit is a useful deliverable packaged for a reachable buyer. We cannot copy an established founder's audience or assume similar demand. Start with a small paid output; productize repeated work after buyers demonstrate interest. Avoid treating early launch sales as recurring revenue.

## 2. Larry: distribution for an existing product

[Postiz's account](https://postiz.com/blog/ai-builders-postiz-98k-month-millions-views) describes Oliver Henry's agent producing app-marketing content and using analytics to improve it. A [creator interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OV5eK91YY68) is available. Postiz sells the infrastructure involved, so its success story is interested evidence. The original X post could not be retrieved directly in this review. We did not independently verify app receipts or assign revenue causally to Larry.

Our inference: use one existing product, one conversion event and a controlled content experiment. Track paying customers rather than views. Human review and publishing effort, image-generation costs and platform fees belong in the economics. For Adam, agents prepare drafts; Adam handles external communication.

## 3. XBOW: output verification, with financial limits

[XBOW's technical account](https://xbow.com/blog/top-1-how-xbow-did-it) explains automated vulnerability discovery, validators and human review before submission. [HackerOne corroborates its reputation ranking](https://www.hackerone.com/blog/ai-hackbots-security-testing-update). This is evidence of real technical performance. Neither source establishes an audited net bounty profit for an individual owner, and not every valid report earns a bounty.

Our inference: adopt a worker-plus-verifier pattern for ordinary client software, where a test can reject a plausible but incorrect output. Do not infer easy personal security income from an enterprise research system. No third-party security testing was performed for this study.

## 4. Claudius / Project Vend: operations improve, autonomy remains limited

Anthropic and Andon Labs ran a real office shop. [Phase one](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-1) failed commercially. [Phase two](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-2) reported improved margins after better tools, cost visibility and operating procedures; significant human support remained. The added CEO agent was not clearly responsible for improvement.

Our inference: give the system visible unit costs and explicit procedures before adding management agents. Role separation earns its place when it resolves a bottleneck. A real shop experiment also differs from simulated Vending-Bench performance.

## What does not establish agent income

- [SWE-Lancer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12115) maps solved benchmark tasks to historical freelance prices. It does not show those benchmark runs collecting payments from new clients.
- x402 and other payment rails enable transactions. Their existence does not establish demand for our service.
- Token price appreciation, airdrops and treasury valuations do not establish operating profit from useful paid work.
- A vendor's revenue from selling agent software does not automatically prove that its customers' agents are profitable.

## The throughline we should test

This is our synthesis, not a demonstrated causal law: demand → bounded output → independent check → collected payment → cost and customer feedback → repeatable delivery.

The cases suggest five practical requirements:

1. Distribution exists before scaling production. A named buyer or a reachable audience matters more than a large agent roster.
2. The output has economic value independently of being generated by an agent.
3. A verifier can detect wrong work. Self-reported completion is insufficient.
4. Revenue and costs are observable. Include failed attempts, revisions, refunds and owner time.
5. Specialize only where repeated work or bottlenecks justify another role.

For Earnbots, the recommended first test remains one scoped AI integration for an existing web app. This recommendation matches our existing skills and requires neither speculative trading capital nor paid proposal credits. The proposed $500 fee is a pricing hypothesis, not evidence of demand. Adam supplies the human buyer conversation and commitments. Agents research, draft, implement, test and prepare the handoff.

## Roles for the first paid workflow

- Researcher: produce a brief with buyer, problem, evidence, scope and unanswered questions. Do not contact buyers.
- Builder: implement the agreed workflow in a reviewable branch after assignment is secured.
- Verifier: check acceptance criteria independently, test error paths and record failures. May reject delivery.
- Owner: Adam conducts communication, approves commitments and confirms acceptance/payment evidence.

These are roles, not a requirement to run four persistent agents. Start sequentially. Add concurrency only when it shortens a demonstrated bottleneck without increasing rework.

## Evidence to record before claiming success

Keep a private receipt ledger: payment date, amount actually collected, fees, refunds, model and infrastructure costs, owner minutes, revision time, scope, verification result and repeat-order interest. Never publish customer identities, receipts or sensitive project data without permission.

Cash contribution = collected receipts − refunds − payment/platform fees − direct model/tool/infrastructure costs. Owner time is reported separately; subtract its agreed value to estimate economic profit. Do not describe work as literally risk-free or infer profit when a cost is missing.

The experiment brief is [available here](./first-experiment.md). The first win is an accepted, paid result with recorded costs. Repeatability requires subsequent evidence, not one enthusiastic launch.
